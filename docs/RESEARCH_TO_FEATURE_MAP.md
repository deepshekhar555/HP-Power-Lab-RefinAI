# Research-to-Feature Map

This is a focused translation of selected research into features supported by the project's public benchmark data. It is not a systematic review and does not establish HPCL or refinery performance.

## Research translated into the prototype

| Research | Method objective | Implementation | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Qin (2012), [Survey on data-driven industrial process monitoring and diagnosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arcontrol.2012.09.004) | Survey data-driven process monitoring and diagnosis approaches for industrial processes. | The CCPP sample view reports input completeness, reference-range support, and multivariate PCA monitoring statistics rather than treating the prediction residual as a stand-alone diagnosis. | The UCI records are shuffled and have no fault labels or time order. The app demonstrates statistical monitoring only; it does not diagnose faults or estimate persistence. |
| Jackson and Mudholkar (1979), [Control procedures for residuals associated with principal component analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1979.10489779) | Monitor retained principal-component variation and residual variation after PCA. | Fit-only PCA retains enough components for at least 95% explained variance. Hotelling T-squared and Q residual scores use separate calibration data for 99th-percentile reference thresholds. The UI reports the per-sample scores, threshold comparisons, and largest statistical feature contributions. | On this fixed CCPP split, 3 components retain 97.45% variance; the test-set reference-flag rates are 1.20% for T-squared and 1.04% for Q. These rates are not accuracy or recall: no anomaly labels are available. |
| Jardine, Lin, and Banjevic (2006), [A review on machinery diagnostics and prognostics implementing condition-based maintenance](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymssp.2005.09.012) | Review condition-based maintenance, including diagnostics and remaining-useful-life prognostics. | The existing project includes an engine-held-out C-MAPSS RUL benchmark. The dashboard also adds a local accept/defer/reject review ledger to demonstrate a human review step. | C-MAPSS is simulated aircraft-engine degradation data, not refinery equipment. Browser feedback is illustrative, is not sent to a plant or server, and is not used as model training labels. |
| Angelopoulos and Bates, [A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511) | Introduce distribution-free prediction sets/intervals under exchangeability assumptions. | CCPP uses a reserved calibration split and absolute-residual split conformal to display sample prediction intervals. | The 90% interval achieved 91.27% empirical coverage on the fixed held-out CCPP test split. Coverage is not guaranteed under distribution shift and is not refinery calibration. |
| Fisher, Rudin, and Dominici (2019), [All Models Are Wrong, but Many Are Useful: Variable Importance for Black-Box, Proprietary, or Misspecified Prediction Models, using Model Class Reliance](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2018.1527006) | Examine model reliance on variables through predictive-performance changes under feature perturbations. | The CCPP Gradient Boosting replay independently shuffles each held-out input 15 times and reports the change in MAE with repeat variability. | This simple permutation diagnostic is model- and split-specific, not a causal effect or the paper's full Model Class Reliance method. Correlated inputs can mask or share importance, and independent shuffling can create unrealistic combinations. |
| Saxena et al. (2008), [Damage Propagation Modeling for Aircraft Engine Run-to-Failure Simulation](https://ti.arc.nasa.gov/tech/dash/groups/pcoe/prognostic-data-repository/) | Provide simulated run-to-failure engine data for prognostics research. | The existing C-MAPSS workflow selects a model on engine-disjoint validation and evaluates it against the supplied FD001 test engines/RUL labels. | Dataset scope is aircraft-engine simulation; scores cannot be transferred to HPCL assets without site-specific data and validation. |

## Why some common research features are not claimed here

- **CUSUM/EWMA, drift detection, alarm persistence, and warning lead time** require ordered timestamps. The CCPP release used here has none, so replay order must not be treated as a process timeline.
- **C-MAPSS RUL prediction intervals** are not added from terminal training rows: those rows have RUL zero by construction, while the official test endpoints are right-censored and carry positive remaining-life labels. Calibrating on the terminal training rows would not represent the test-time prediction task. A credible interval experiment needs a documented censoring-matched, engine-disjoint calibration design.
- **Fault classification, detection precision/recall, and supervised diagnosis** require labeled faults or maintenance events. The public benchmark split has no such labels.
- **Operating-regime-specific baselines** require plant regime labels or a validated regime-identification study. PCA components and input-percentile ranges are not regime labels.
- **Root-cause or causal explanation** cannot be obtained from correlation, PCA contribution, or predictive feature contribution alone. The shown contributions are descriptive statistics, not causal attributions.
- **Operator effectiveness, safety impact, savings, and emissions impact** require an approved, read-only site pilot with human review, site factors, and a pre-agreed evaluation design.

## Reproducibility

Run `python ml/train_real_models.py` to regenerate the benchmark model metrics, calibrated intervals, PCA reference statistics, replay JSON, and plots. The split seeds, calibration partitions, thresholds, and test diagnostics are saved in `ml/models/metrics.json`; per-sample fields are exported to `dashboard/ccpp_replay.json`.
